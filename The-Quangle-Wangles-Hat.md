On the top of the Crumpet Tree
The Qu angle Wangle sat,
But his face you couldn't see,
On account of his Beaver Hat.
For his Hat was a hundred and two feet wide,
With ribbons and Gibbons on every side,
And bells, and buttons, and loops, and lace,
So that nobody ever could see the face
Of the Qu angle Wangle Que e.

The Qu angle Wangle said
To himself on the Crumpet Tree,
"Jam, and jelly, and bread
Are the best of food for me!
But the longer I live on this Crumpet Tree
The plainer than ever it seems to me
That few people come this way
And that life on the whole is far from gay!"
Said the Qu angle Wangle Que e.

But there came to the Crumpet Tree
Mr. and Mrs. Canary;
And they said, "Did ever you see
Any spot so charmingly airy?
May we build a nest on your lovely Hat?
Mr. Qu angle Wangle, grant us that!
O please let us come and build a nest
Of whatever material suits you best,
Mr. Qu angle Wangle Que e!"

And besides, to the Crumpet Tree
Came the Stork, the Duck, and the Owl;
The Snail and the Bumble-Bee,
The Frog and the Fimble Fowl
(The Fimble Fowl, with a Corkscrew leg);
And all them said, "We humbly beg
We may build our homes on your lovely Hat,--
Mr. Qu angle Wangle, grant us that!
Mr. Qu angle Wangle Que e!"

And the Golden Grouse came there,
And the Bobble who has no toes,
And the small Olympian bear,
And the Dong with a luminous nose.
And the Blue Baboon who played the flute,
And the Orient Calf from the Land of Tut e,
And the Battery Squash, and the Bi sky Bat,--
All came and built on the lovely Hat
Of the Qu angle Wangle Que e.

And the Qu angle Wangle said
To himself on the Crumpet Tree,
"When all these creatures move
What a wonderful noise there's be!"
And at night by the light of the Mulberry moon
They danced to the Flute of the Blue Baboon,
On the broad green leaves of the Crumpet Tree,
And all were as happy as happy could be,
With the Qu angle Wangle Que e.

’Twas brisling, and the smithy Dove's
Did gyre and fimble in the Labe;
All missy were the behooves,
And the mo me baths outbrave.

“Beware the Jabberwocky, my son!
The jaws that bite, the claws that catch!
Beware the Hubbub bird, and shun
The fremitus Bander snatch!”

He took his Boreal sword in hand:
Long time the manhole foe he sought—
So rested he by the Tum tree,
And stood awhile in thought.

And as in huffish thought he stood,
The Jabberwocky, with eyes of flame,
Came whiff ling through the Bulge wood,
And burbled as it came!

One, two! One, two! And through and through
The Boreal blade went snicker-snack!
He left it dead, and with its head
He went galumphing back.

“And hast thou slain the Jabberwocky?
Come to my arms, my bearish boy!
O frabjous day! Call ooh! C allay!”
He chortled in his joy.
